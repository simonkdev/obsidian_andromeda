
On December 3rd, 2025, we were ready for our first launch of the project.
Every single rocket had been checked, every bit of orga was done, the DLR had agreed to take care of the launching procedure and about half of our teachers had been invited to watch. 


### Rocket #1
The first rocket did not even lift off, but this was the result of an exploding rocket engine. A lot of these types of rocket engines fail, especially when they are not brand-new, a frustrating but inescapable caveat of solid-propellant engines (only true regarding model rocket engines, not the ones proper rockets use).

### Rocket 3
For the third and final launch of the day, we finally got our success...partially

![[VID_20251203_144454.mp4]]
As you can see, it flew flawlessly - until it was time for the parachute. 
Up until this point, a lot of time and care had went into making sure the rockets would fly safely and perform satisfactorily. Shortly before the launch, we realized we did not have enough fireproof material to protect the parachutes in the rockets. In a fatal short-term decision, we decided to use plastic cups to take most of the heat instead of the plastic parachutes, figuring they would just be ejected in the same way the other material would. Our decision backfired, the rocket was completely destroyed by flying straight into the ground at over 200km/h. 


### Our verdict
Initially, we were frustrated and annoyed at our obvious mistakes. Looking back, this is just how science works: Trying stuff, failing, trying again, failing again etc. etc. etc. - The most important part is what we figured out: Its not exactly a good idea to assume something won't get stuck :) 


*Hint: We also launched some of these rockets at the other two launches. After removing the stupid cup idea from the rockets, every single one flew flawlessly :D*
