### General:
Since every point on a wavefront emits a wavelet (see [[Huygens-Fresnel principle]]), when [[electromagnetic waves]] (or any kind of waves, really), pass around an obstacle, these wavelets spread around the area they normally would not reach with a straight line due to [[interference]] with wavelets originating from other points of the wave. You could also say the wavelets distribute the wave's momentum directionally.


### Single-slit diffraction
If light passes through one narrow passage, the wavelets originating from infinitely many points the wavefront create [[interference]] with each other, creating one central, bright spot and a gradient of continuously darker light originating from it (this is observable when shining a light through a narrow hole, the light on the other side will be broader than the hole due to diffraction)

### Double-slit diffraction
When light passes through two closely spaced narrow slits, their waves resulting from single-slit diffraction create [[interference]] with each other, resulting in many equally spaced bright and dark spots ("interference fringes").

### Size of the obstacle
If the object is small enough, its shadow will therefore be visibly smaller than the object itself - on the contrary, if it is bigger, the shadow will not have a noticeable difference in size.
