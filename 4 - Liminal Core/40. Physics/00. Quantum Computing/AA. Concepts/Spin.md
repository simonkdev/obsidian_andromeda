### General:
In quantum mechanics, spin describes a magnetic orientation along a specified direction. It can
either be S (South Pole) or N (North Pole), therefore being able to represent a [[bit]].

### Impact of measurements:
Unlike in classical computing, measurements have an impact on the target in [[quantum computing]].
Since we can measure the spin using the [[Stern-Gerlach Apparatus]], we can also observe the impact of measuring it
if we measure multiple times in a row. We do this by positioning multiple magnet pairs along the trajectory of the atom,
since that enables us to measure the spin of atoms which we have measured before. 
Here we can observe the following:
.
	With infinitely many magnet pairs all aligned on the same axis, we will get  the same result over and over again. The measured spin does not change.
.
	With 3 magnet pairs, where the first and third one's directions match, but the second one doesn't, the distribution is always 50/50.  Atoms which gave the result N at the first layer don't have higher chances of giving N at the third layer. Therefore, the measurement of a different direction in between changes the measured spin / result.
