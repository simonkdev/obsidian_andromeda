### Classical Theory:
Classical theory is based on the general concept that everything is deterministic. If you have *infinite precision of measurements,* you are able to predict anything with *unlimited reliability*. 

Since, in reality, we do not have infinite precision, we will always have some small error. This error scales over time though, which is the reason why weather forecasts become more unreliable the further in the future they aim to predict.
Therefore, classical theory says that the answer to any question is already determined before we ask it, and asking it does not do anything besides access this determined value.

### Quantum Theory:
Quantum theory says that the answer to any question is not determined until you ask it. You can predict the *probabilities* of the different possible answers, but you can *not reliably predict* which answer it will be.  

For example, measuring the [[Spin]] state of an electron determines the state by making the measurement, but you can not reliably predict which state it will be beforehand.
