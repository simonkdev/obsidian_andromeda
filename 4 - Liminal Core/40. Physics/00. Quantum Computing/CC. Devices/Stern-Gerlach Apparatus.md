### General:
The Stern-Gerlach Apparatus is a device used to measure the [[Spin]] of an atom.


### Structure:
Consists of a single atom source which propels atoms forward in a straight line, and two magnets positioned 
on opposite sides of the atoms trajectory. Both lie on the same axis we want to measure the spin on (e.g. horizontally).
They have opposite poles, meaning one faces the trajectory with its south and the other with its north pole.
One of them is located closer to the trajectory than the other. Looking from the perspective of the source, there is a 
screen fit for detecting impacting atoms behind the magnets.

![[Pasted image 20260124115502.png]]
### The experiment:
Now, atoms are emitted from the source and propelled on a straight line. 

If the [[Spin]] of the atom along the desired axis aligns with the magnets (e.g. the south pole of the magnets being right next to the south pole of the atom),
the atom will be deflected by both magnets. Since one of them is closer to the other, it exerts a greater force and the atom is deflected in the direction 
of the other magnet. This results in the atom impacting onto the screen on the side of the weaker magnet.

If the [[Spin]] of the atom along the desired axis doesn't align with the magnets, it is attracted by both of them, which results in an impact on the side
of the stronger magnet because, again, it exerts a greater force.

With a very high number of atoms, a 50/50 distribution between alignment and misalignment becomes apparent, showing that [[Spin]] is entirely random.

### Multiple magnet arrays:
If we extend the device to more than one magnet pair so that atoms exiting either side will land in yet another magnet pair, we can observe the impact
of measurements on [[Spin]].
