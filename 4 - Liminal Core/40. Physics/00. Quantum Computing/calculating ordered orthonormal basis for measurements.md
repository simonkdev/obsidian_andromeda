### General:
The orthonormal basis for measuring spin in specific directions can be derived from the angle it is rotated relative to the Z-Axis.

### Universal definition:
The basis corresponding to a measurement along an axis rotated $\theta$° *from the Z-Axis (vertical)* in real space is defined as follows:
$$
\left( \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\theta/2) \\ -\sin(\theta/2)\end{bmatrix} , \begin{bmatrix} \sin(\theta/2) \\ \cos(\theta/2)\end{bmatrix}\right)
$$
Notice the angle is cut in half. This is because the formula uses the angle $\alpha$ at which the vectors are turned, and since a vector describing spin in direction 90° (physical space) *inversely describes the spin* in direction 270°, we only need half the rotations. This is why the *real rotational angle* is cut *in half* to represent the *vector's rotational angle*. 
For example, if we make a measurement along the horizontal real axis, we insert $\theta = 90°$ and receive the cos/sin values corresponding to 45° since $\alpha = \theta/2 = 45°$.
