### General:
The Copenhagen interpretation describes quantum theory with states jumping to basis states based on given possibilities. 

Quantum theory says:

> The [result of]  any [measurement] is not determined until you ask it. You can predict the *probabilities* of the different possible [results], but you can *not reliably predict* which [result] it will be.
	-[[Classical Theory vs. Quantum Theory]]> 

But it does not explain what actually happens. The Copenhagen interpretation does: Spin states jump to basis spin states correlating to the measured direction of [[Spin]].


### Entanglement in the Copenhagen interpretation:
Here, you already know the state of the electron once you measure one of the entangled bits, because their combined state just jumped to the measured result. 
Once you measure the other electron, its state is already determined.

This is another central idea of the Copenhagen interpretation: When you measure one of two entangled qubits, both immediately jump to the same state.
