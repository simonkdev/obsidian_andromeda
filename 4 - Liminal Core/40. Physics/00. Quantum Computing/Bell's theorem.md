### General:
Bell's theorem and the experiments based on it are the generally accepted proof of the [[Copenhagen interpretation of quantum theory]]. The core idea is that classical theory and quantum theory predict different results for this specific experiment and by observing its result, we can prove or debunk either theory.

### The experiment:
If you have a very long string of pairs of entangled qubits, split them into two strings (with each pair being separated of course), measure their states in one direction randomly chosen out of three  and then compare your results, the two theories predict different outcomes.

### Classical Answer:
Since we assume that every single result can be determined, we can estimate the share of results that will be the same for both strings. The full description and calculation is in the first reference below, but drastically summed up it works like this:
- We assume that some deterministic action chooses between the eight possible configurations for the three directions.
- We then look at the nine possible combinations of the direction chosen for string one and the direction chosen for string two respectively
- We find that for every possible configuration, at least five of the nine combinations will result in the two strings matching their results.
From this, we can predict that at least 5/9 times, the result of both strings matches.

### Quantum Answer:
We assume that every time the qubits are measured in the same direction, the strings will match because the result will be identical. This occurs 1/3 times since we choose randomly out of three directions.
If we do not measure in the same direction, we still know its state in the direction we measured the first qubit in. If we  rewrite the term describing the state of the second qubit while knowing its state in a different direction, we will find that 1/4 times, they will have the same result (full mathematical proof in the first reference).
Now, using these, we can calculate our prediction for the share of matching results as $(1/3 * 1) + (2/3 * 1/4) = 1/2$.

### The experiment:
With many, continuously more sophisticated approaches, the prediction based on the quantum theory has proven as the correct one every single time.
